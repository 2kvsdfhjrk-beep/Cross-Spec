The problem

Most tipster tables mark their own homework.

Strike rates and profit figures usually come out of the same database the tipster posts into. Losing tips get quietly tidied. Screenshots get cropped. There's no outside anchor, so "consistent profit" ends up meaning whatever the site would like it to mean.

Edited after the off

A record you can rewrite isn't a record. Change the odds, nudge the selection, and yesterday looks a lot smarter than it was.

Losers quietly pruned

Delete the bad month and the average leaps. Survivorship does the marketing for you, and the reader never sees the bin.

Nothing to check against

No independent odds, no independent results, no timestamp anyone else can see. You're asked to take the number on trust.

How we verify

Three layers, and none of them ask you to trust us.

A tip is captured, settled and published by machinery that no human on this site can reach into. That's the whole product. Everything else is decoration.

1

Stamped on arrival

The server clock times your tip — never your device. Odds are pulled from an independent feed and frozen at that instant. Post after the event has started and it's rejected outright.

2

Settled by machine

Results arrive from a feed on a schedule. Settlement is a job, not a button. No tipster can mark their own bet a winner, and neither can we.

3

Published proof

Each day every tip is hashed into a single root and published openly. Anyone can check that a tip existed, exactly as stated, on that date — and hasn't moved since.

A losing tip is published exactly as loudly as a winning one.

Records are append-only. There is no edit, and there is no delete — not for tipsters, not for admins. If something genuinely needs correcting, we publish a void that points back at the original. The original stays exactly where it is, for good.

We also keep the raw settlement payload behind every result, so when a result is disputed there's evidence to look at rather than a shrug.

House rules

The table is built to be un-farmable.

The moment a leaderboard confers status, somebody tries to game it. These rules are in the code from day one, not in a policy page.

Ranked on ROI, not profit

Return per unit staked. Firing out 400 tips a month buys you nothing.

One unit, every time

Flat staking is enforced. No quietly sizing up on the ones you fancy to bury a slump.

100 settled tips minimum

Below the threshold you don't appear in a ranking at all. Hot streaks aren't records.

Ranges, not single numbers

A 40-tip run shows as the wide, uncertain band it actually is. Confidence intervals, on the page, by default.

Odds bands

Ranked within bands, so month-end lottery tickets at 40/1 can't run away with the table.

One human, one account

Device and account fingerprinting, so nobody runs twelve handles and shows you the lucky one.

The record

We'd rather show you an honest range than a flattering number.

Point estimates make thin records look certain. Every table on BetStable shows the interval, the sample size, and the drawdown alongside the headline — because that's what tells you whether a record means anything yet.
